# SESSION-0011 — 2026-08-02

## Objective

Record the owner's acceptance of `SESSION-0010` and resolve the three issues it
opened — including the one blocking the M2 manifests.

## Acceptance recorded

**`ACCEPT-0006`** — the Project Owner accepted the `SESSION-0010` decisions at
revision `a87ce51`. Those revisions are now `Active`.

The rationale endorses the mode of working: **extracting reusable architectural
patterns instead of solving isolated cases**. `ADR-0031` is the first `Active`

and the acceptance makes that a standard rather than a one-off.

## Decisions made

**`ADR-0032` — Registry Specification versus Registry Projection.** The
specification is authoritative and defines identity, purpose, ownership,
membership rules, required metadata, constraints, relationships and extension
rules. The projection is derived: the generated index of entities currently
registered. The projection is browsed; the specification governs. Resolves
`ISSUE-0053`.

**`ADR-0033` — a ProcessPolicy governs a Workflow.** Independent artifact types.
A Workflow references ProcessPolicies; a ProcessPolicy never embeds execution; a
Workflow never embeds normative rules. Resolves `ISSUE-0051`.

**`ADR-0034` — the Knowledge Explorer is a per-repository projection.** Not
documentation. A first-class generated product of the Knowledge Compiler that
every adopting repository generates over its own domain. Resolves `ISSUE-0052`.

## What was learned

**`ADR-0032` resolved a three-way contradiction without superseding anything.**
That is the notable part. `ISSUE-0053` had three `Active` ADRs saying different
things and assumed one would have to yield. Splitting the overloaded word left
all three true: `ADR-0016` because generated indexes are still derived,
`ADR-0031` because registries are still first-class, `ADR-0012` because the
specification is validated while the projection is generated.

**The issue's own proposed reconciliation was rejected, for a reason worth
keeping.** `ISSUE-0053` had suggested testing whether a registry *restates*
content held elsewhere. That makes an artifact's kind depend on a content
comparison that shifts as specifications evolve — so an artifact could change
kind without being edited. A structural distinction beat a content test.

**Fifth vocabulary collision, resolved the same way as the previous four.**
"Skill", "authoritative", "state", "policy", now "registry". Every one was an
overloaded term split into two named concepts. The recurrence is itself the
finding: the discipline is being applied *after* naming rather than before, and
`ADR-0032` records that observation rather than treating this as another
one-off.

**`ADR-0032` named what the existing hand-maintained indexes are.**
`governance/issues/index.md` and `governance/adr/README.md` are Registry
Projections maintained by hand because no compiler exists. Their Registry
Specifications do not exist either. `ISSUE-0037`'s register now has vocabulary
for what it tracks.

**`ADR-0033`'s stated purpose is sharper than the distinction it draws.** The
separation exists to prevent *implementation procedures from becoming the source
of engineering policy*. A rule living only inside the steps that implement it
cannot be reviewed, reused, or changed without editing an executable artifact.
That is the third instance of the same shape in this project — after `ADR-0029`
(ADRs historical, policies normative) and `ADR-0032` (specification governing,
projection generated): **the normative artifact is always distinct from the thing
it governs.**

**`ADR-0034` turns the Knowledge Explorer into a shipped capability.** Every
adopting repository generates one over its own domain, which is plausibly the
most visible benefit of adoption — and the first deliverable a non-engineer
stakeholder would ever see. It also sits behind the toolchain: `ADR-0017`
guarantees adopters need nothing installed to *consume*, but generating an
Explorer is generation, which is exactly the carve-out.

**Second correction without a mechanism.** `ADR-0032` corrects `ADR-0031`'s
opening definition, as `ADR-0026` corrected `ADR-0025`'s examples. Both are
visible only in prose and the ADR index Corrections table. `ISSUE-0048` now has
two instances rather than one.

**M2 and M3 are both unblocked, for the first time since M1.** No open issue
blocks a named deliverable.
