## Decisions made

**`ADR-0065` — Descriptive and Operational entities.** Every Layer A entity
belongs to exactly one family. Descriptive entities describe knowledge and are
owned by a BoundedContext; operational entities describe engineering activity and
are owned by the engineering process. **They remain in one metamodel and are not
peers.**

This was raised as a recommendation, not a blocker, and taken anyway. Under
`ADR-0062` the default for a non-blocking question is deferral — but the test is
whether the answer changes what gets built next, and a top-level abstraction
changes the shape of every remaining specification. Twelve entities needed a
one-line declaration; thirty-two would have needed re-reading.

## What was learned

### `Relationship` was absent from every list this project produced

Twenty-two sessions, sixty-five decisions, several inventories — and nothing
named the edge. `ADR-0042` decided classification was a relationship rather than
a property in `SESSION-0016`; the general entity that decision implies was never
written down.

It appeared only because the build order was changed to put the semantic
backbone first. **The graph is the model, and the model had no term for the
graph's edges.**

### The OWL checkpoint paid for itself immediately

Writing the specifications as class definitions found, within one file, six
things that four separate Markdown documents had hidden:

1. **`Relationship` competes with OWL's own object properties.** The metamodel
   now has two ways to express an edge and does not say which is authoritative.
   The likely answer is that they sit at different abstraction levels
   (`ADR-0043`) — but that is not what the specification says. **The finding most
   likely to change an entity**, and much cheaper now than after every
   operational entity is written.
2. **`Dimension` may be redundant.** As a class it is 1:1 with its specification
   and carries no property the specification lacks. The Markdown said so; reading
   it as OWL made the consequence unavoidable.
3. **A missing superclass.** `Concept`, `Capability`, `Invariant` and `Actor`
   each independently defined identity as a qualified name within a
   BoundedContext. Four identical restrictions is the standard signal, and the
   skeleton introduces `ContextualEntity`, which appears in no specification.
   **The repetition was invisible across four files and obvious in one.**
4. **`Evidence.supports` has no range.** "Any assertion" is not a class. What an
   assertion *is* turns out to be `ADR-0060`'s Interpretive/Mechanical boundary,
   which lives in prose and not in the model.
5. `Relationship.typedBy` points at nothing defined; OWL forced a provisional
   guess where Markdown allowed the question to stay open.
6. **`DimensionAssignment.hasValue` is not expressible in plain OWL** — its range
   depends on which Dimension. A genuine expressiveness limit, and the first
   concrete evidence of what the compiler must check that an ontology cannot.

The recommendation was to generate the skeleton early because it would expose
simplifications sooner. It did, at 44% completion, and every one of the six would
have been more expensive later.

### The split was already there

**All twelve specified entities are descriptive.** The operational family is
entirely blank.

Nobody planned that. The build order followed dependency, and dependency
followed the family boundary without anyone having named it. `ADR-0065` is that
boundary arriving after the fact — which is a better outcome than inventing it,
because the evidence preceded the decision.

### A predicted contradiction surfaced and did not block

`ISSUE-0073` was flagged in `SESSION-0021` as the debt most likely to surface
during B1. It surfaced in `Evidence`: the inherited ranking puts runtime
observation at the top, while `ADR-0061` places Operational Knowledge outside
the model entirely.

**It was recorded and stepped over.** A citation can reference an observation the
model does not own. That is `ADR-0062` operating exactly as intended — the
contradiction is real, and it is not yet an obstacle.
