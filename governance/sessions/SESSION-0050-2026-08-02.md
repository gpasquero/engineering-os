## What was learned

### Guidance had to be measured on what did *not* change

The obvious version of this benchmark — *is the advice the same after ten
commits?* — measures nothing. **Guidance should change when the system changes.**
Advice that ignored nine commits of real work would be preserving staleness.

The only version that means anything is narrower: **for a subject whose evidence
nobody touched, is the recommended work the same?**

The first implementation of that filter was a substring match and left **two
untouched subjects out of ten** — a denominator small enough to have produced a
meaningless 100 %. Resolving each delta key to its actual source path gave
**eight**, and the reading changed from `100 %` to `80 %`.

**The result was one sloppy comparison away from being flattering and useless.**

### The third appearance of one authoring error

Both changed pairs are `R-discover`, on two artifacts nothing touched in ten
commits.

`R-discover` declares `applies-to: [Artifact]` — subject-specific advice — and
one of its steps uses `Q-unsupported`, declared **`subject: none`**. That step
returns the same list for every subject, and **the list grows as the model
grows**.

**Two untouched artifacts received different advice because the model had grown
around them.**

`EQ-09` scored 216/216 for the same reason. `EQ-05` mixed the same way. **Each
time a new consumer was built, the same mistake was waiting in a new place** —
and each time it was found by the new consumer rather than by a check.

`tools/check-plans.py` now reports it. Whether the step belongs there is the
author's call; not being able to see it was not.

### Prose is not guidance

A recommendation is fingerprinted as its status plus, per action, the sorted ids
of the work it names. **Two recommendations naming the same work in different
words are the same guidance.**

This is `ADR-0134` applied one level down: the contract is what a team would
**do**, and every layer of wording between the model and the action is
implementation.

### Naming a property without measuring it was the harder discipline

Decision Preservation is the promise, it is nameable in one sentence, and
inventing a number for it would have taken an hour.

**Every such number would measure agreement with our own recommendations** — an
internal oracle for a property settled externally, by what happened next. It
would score 100 % on stable wrong advice, which is precisely what `ADR-0132` was
written to prevent.

What is recorded instead is **how it could become measurable**: a commit that
follows another commit is a decision a real team made, and history says which
ones were reverted. That oracle is outside the system and it already exists.

### Deterministic Discovery is finished, and the evidence says so

Six milestones of optimization, two repositories, 299 and 453 proposals, no
metamodel change — and `EQ-01` answers **nothing** in either. `wa-b2b` holds 135
documents and 369 migrations no rule can read.

**More rules will not reach them.** The reviewer's instruction to stop is
supported by this project's own measurements.
