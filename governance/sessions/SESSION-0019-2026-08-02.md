# SESSION-0019 — 2026-08-02

## Objective

Record the owner's acceptance of `SESSION-0018`, resolve the two gate questions
it raised, and record the hierarchy that explains why ADRs, Policies and Gates
all exist.

## Acceptance recorded

**`ACCEPT-0014`** — the Project Owner accepted the `SESSION-0018` decisions at
revision `206a7cd`. Those revisions are now `Active`.

The rationale reads the previous session's difficulty as the intended behaviour
arriving: **the project's own architectural rules are beginning to govern the
evolution of the architecture itself.** `SESSION-0018` had reported the
metamodel-first gate binding on a needed concept as a problem; the reviewer
reads it as the system working.

## Decisions made

**`ADR-0054` — Engineering Gate is a first-class metamodel concept.** A Gate is a
review process applied to the introduction or modification of an architectural
concept, defining seven fields. The Metamodel Position Gate, Dimension Review,
Artifact Definition Review and a future Compiler Impact Review become instances.
The metamodel models Gate independently from the rules a gate executes. Resolves
`ISSUE-0067`.

**`ADR-0055` — questions belong to Gates, not to artifacts.** Supersedes
`ADR-0038`. Every Gate declares which questions apply, so a purely semantic
concept never encounters a compiler question. Resolves `ISSUE-0068`.

**`ADR-0056` — `Principle → Policy → Process → Artifact`.** Three levels of
engineering knowledge, explaining why ADRs, Policies and Gates exist without
overlapping responsibilities.

## What was learned

**The reframing pattern recurred, and this time the category was the answer.**
`ISSUE-0067` asked how to classify one review and offered three options. All
three were answers to the wrong question: the observation that *multiple gates
had evolved independently* is itself the finding, and Gate is what the metamodel
needs. Dimension Review is an instance.

That is the fifth time an issue's candidate answers were all rejected in favour
of reframing — after `ISSUE-0053`, `ISSUE-0056`, `ISSUE-0058` and `ISSUE-0066`.
The pattern named in `SESSION-0015` holds: **when an issue's options all share an
assumption, the assumption is the issue.**

**The deadlock dissolved rather than being resolved.** `ISSUE-0067` recorded that
`ADR-0035`'s gate could not be satisfied for Dimension Review because the
metamodel does not exist. Under `ADR-0054` the question never arises: Dimension
Review is not a new artifact type, it is an instance of one. The gate binds on
`Gate`, once.

**`ADR-0055` took the deeper reading rather than the narrower fix.**
`ISSUE-0068` had offered `None (Not Applicable)` — extending `ADR-0039`'s
precedent — as the likely answer. It was rejected because it would still ask the
question. Moving questions to Gates removes the encounter entirely.

**Enforcement moved, and became thinner.** Universality was crude but hard to
evade. Now a gate applies only when triggered, so **triggering conditions are
the entire enforcement surface** — and `ADR-0054` lists that as a field without
saying what may appear in one. A concept could pass every triggered gate while a
gate that should have triggered did not.

**`ADR-0056` closes a loop opened three sessions ago.** `ADR-0046` recorded that
three ADRs applying one naming discipline meant the discipline was a rule
belonging somewhere other than an ADR. It now has a level: Policy, derived from
a Principle, implemented by a Process.

**Seventh terminology collision, and the second to arrive inside an organizing
ADR.** `ADR-0056` uses "Level" for a second scheme after Abstraction Level — the
qualified name `ADR-0046` introduced *specifically so that "level" would not be
ambiguous* — and "Process" for a second concept after `ADR-0033`'s. Both
recorded in `ISSUE-0069` before the metamodel can inherit them.

**Three named principles are currently `Active` ADRs.** `ADR-0056` gives
examples — the Registry Pattern, `Definition → Instance → Assignment`, semantic
versus compilation architecture — which are `ADR-0031`, `ADR-0052` and
`ADR-0053`. Whether Principle is an artifact type is unstated, and `ADR-0029`'s
argument applies with more force to principles than to policies: they are the
most stable and most reused content, and currently the most deeply buried in the
decision history. `ISSUE-0070`.
