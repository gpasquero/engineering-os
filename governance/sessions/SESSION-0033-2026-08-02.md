# SESSION-0033 — 2026-08-02

## Objective

Record acceptance of `SESSION-0032` and **exploit** the Kubernetes model rather
than build another one: answer questions a maintainer would actually ask,
classify findings by strength, and produce guidance instead of only knowledge.

## Decisions made

**`ADR-0089` — engineering value is the optimization target.** Every milestone
answers *what valuable engineering capability became possible?* — not *what
architectural capability*. The fourth and narrowest admission test; the
difference from `ADR-0084` is the word **valuable**.

**`ADR-0090` — the finding taxonomy.** Eight kinds ranked by the strength of the
claim they license. **Evidence quality is expressed through provenance and
support, never a score** — engineering evidence is not probabilistic, and a
number answers *how much* while destroying *why*.

**`ADR-0091` — Engineering Recommendation.** Guidance composed entirely of
semantic queries. **Not AI workflow selection**: the semantic layer recommends
engineering actions and execution engines — a developer, an agent, a pipeline —
consume them. Engineering OS stays independent of any AI runtime.

## What was learned

### A query found something nobody noticed while authoring

`Q-assumptions` on `Artifact.ConflictGo` returns **empty**. The same query on
`Artifact.MetaV1Types` returns two invariants.

**Nothing in the model constrains `Concept.Conflict`** — and conflict detection
is the behaviour of this subsystem most likely to surprise a user. Classified a
**traceability gap**, rank 4, the strongest finding of the session.

It was produced by asking a question, not by re-reading the sources. That is the
first time this project has found something by *querying* rather than by
*writing*.

### Two of three new queries return nothing, and that is correct

*Which decisions became obsolete but are still reflected in code?* and *which
artifacts no longer match their design rationale?* both return **empty** on
Kubernetes, because **no KEP in the modelled scope is superseded**.

**Empty is a true answer, not a failure**, and the queries are exercised by
`tests/projects/has-path`, which mentions no domain. Making them return something
by enriching the Kubernetes model would have been fabrication.

### The second gap was in the query language, and the first was not

External validation has now found two gaps, and **neither was about Kubernetes**:

- the **authoring format** could not carry a source URI;
- the **query language** could not filter a row on a property several hops away
  while still returning the row.

**The metamodel has needed no change across two milestones.** Twenty-three
entities have modelled a governance repository and an API-machinery subsystem.
What has needed changing is everything around them.

### The taxonomy made the previous session's result smaller

Classifying honestly puts the Kubernetes finding at **rank 5 of 8**, and leaves
ranks 1–3 empty. **The strongest thing that validation found is a documentation
gap.**

That is a worse headline and a better record. A system that reported it without
its rank would be overstating itself in exactly the way `ADR-0088` and `ADR-0090`
exist to prevent.

### The audit turned the finding on itself

`R-audit-model` on Kubernetes returns two items: `BackwardCompatible` and
`TimestampNotUpdatedOnTakeover` have **no recorded enforcement point**.

The second is the finding this project is proudest of — and the audit says
nothing is recorded as enforcing it. **That is exactly what a maintainer should
know about it**, and no one asked the question; a declared recommendation did.
