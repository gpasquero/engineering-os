## Decisions made

**`ADR-0026` — the lifecycle belongs to a Revision.** `ADR-0020` is confirmed
correct. An Artifact is an *identity* that may own many Revisions and **has no
lifecycle of its own** — only metadata: identifier, ownership, revision history.
A Revision has exactly one lifecycle state. The state machine is named after the
entity it governs: `ArtifactRevisionLifecycle`. Resolves `ISSUE-0044`.

**`ADR-0027` — state machines are registered, not enumerated.** No fixed
catalog. Every machine registers nine fields; the framework validates
registrations rather than enumerating lifecycles; the registry is the source of
truth and documentation, visualizations, ontology navigation and validation are
generated from it. The same mechanism serves Engineering OS and every adopting
repository. Resolves `ISSUE-0045`.

## What was learned

**"What state is this artifact in?" is now a malformed question.** That is the
sharpest consequence of `ADR-0026`. Previously the question had a misleading
answer; now it has none, because artifacts do not have states. A whole class of
confusion becomes unaskable rather than merely discouraged — a stronger outcome
than a naming fix.

**The naming rule generalizes further than the issue that prompted it.** "Named
after the entity that owns it" means `WorkflowExecutionLifecycle` and
`CompilerExecutionLifecycle` are unambiguous without explanation, and it forced
the rejection of the shorter `RevisionLifecycle` — other kinds of revision will
exist, and the prefix is what keeps them distinguishable.

**`ISSUE-0045` suspected its own answer and was right.** It had recorded that
"the answer is probably not a list at all, but a registration rule". It was. The
useful part is *why*: because `ADR-0025` applies to target domains, and no
inventory written here could anticipate the state machines a banking system
contains. A rule that reaches beyond the repository cannot be satisfied by a
list maintained inside it.

**`registry over catalogue` is now the second instance of a pattern.**
`MANIFEST.yaml` is already a registry of skills, workflows and contracts, and
`ADR-0027` arrived at the same shape independently for state machines. Recorded
as an observation in `ADR-0027` rather than promoted to a principle — two
instances is a coincidence worth watching, not yet a rule.

**Three of the nine registration fields are unspecified sub-problems.**
`transition rules`, `related ontology concepts` and `authoritative
specification` each need a defined shape before any registration can be written.
The field list makes M2's registry work look smaller than it is; the roadmap now
says so.

**A correction mechanism is missing, and this session needed one.** `ADR-0026`
corrects the *examples* in `ADR-0025` while that ADR's decision stands
untouched. Supersession would misinform — it would signal the state-machine rule
was replaced. Editing would break immutability. The documentation system offers
no third option, so the correction lives in `ADR-0026`'s prose and in a new
Corrections section of the ADR index. Recorded as `ISSUE-0048`.
