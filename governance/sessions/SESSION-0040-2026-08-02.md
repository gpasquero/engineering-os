# SESSION-0040 — 2026-08-02

## Objective

Act on a correction: **`SESSION-0039` measured the limit of the current
deterministic Discovery Workers, not the limit of deterministic Discovery.**

Split Discovery into two stages, record assertion origin, and **test whether the
previous session's conclusion survives.**

## Decisions made

**`ADR-0108` — Discovery has two stages.** Mechanical Discovery produces a
reproducible Mechanical Engineering Model; **Interpretive Discovery reads
exclusively that model** and never opens a source file.

**`ADR-0109` — every proposed assertion records its origin kind**:
`O-mechanical-extraction`, `O-deterministic-rule`,
`O-probabilistic-interpretation`, `O-human-proposal`. **Origin is what the
process was; support is what the evidence is.**

## What was learned

### The conclusion did not survive, and the refutation was in the file

`SESSION-0039` concluded: *deterministic extraction is worse than a human at
abstraction. That is the deterministic ceiling.*

The abstraction the human produced was **already in the repository**:

```text
describe('account lockout & brute-force protection')
  it('locks the account on the 5th wrong password for exactly 15 minutes')
```

Rule `R1` read `it()` names. Rule `R3` reads the `describe` block.

| Human's invariant | `R1` | `R3` |
|---|---|---|
| account lockout | no | **YES** |
| tenant isolation | yes | **YES** |
| refresh token rotation | no | **YES** |
| JWT security | no | **YES** |

**Four of four, with no language model** — including the one reported as *"missed
entirely"* and attributed to a limit of determinism. It was missed because the
rule looked in the wrong place.

**Recorded as a correction in the ADR index**, not as a superseding decision:
`ADR-0107`'s three worker kinds are untouched. What was wrong was a claim about a
measurement, and the mechanism that would have caught it is what `ADR-0108`
builds.

### The split is what makes the measurement possible

Both interpreters read **the same Mechanical Model**, digest `dd47744e6bc66150`.
Without that, `R1` versus `R3` would have been two programs producing different
output for unattributable reasons.

**Before the split, a missing fact and a bad abstraction were the same failure.**
Now they have different owners: `R1`'s failure was interpretation — the fact it
needed was extracted and it did not use it.

### Neither strategy dominates

`R3` reaches the concept and **loses the specific guarantee**.
`Invariant.NoUserEnumeration` — *"wrong password and wrong email return the same
error"* — survives at case level and is absorbed at suite level.

**A rule proposing both is the obvious next deterministic step**, and it is not
built, because the purpose was to measure rather than to keep optimising.

### The argument for a probabilistic interpreter is now weaker, honestly

`SESSION-0039` called the case evidential. **It was not.** The evidence supported
*a better rule*, and a better rule delivered it.

The case must now be made **against `R3`, over the same Mechanical Model** — a
harder bar than the one this project set for itself, and exactly the one
`ADR-0108` exists to enforce.

**Being wrong once about a ceiling is a reason to distrust the next such claim**,
including a future one arguing that determinism has finally run out.

### Origin recording, on its first run

```text
[origin]  {'O-deterministic-rule': 394}
```

**394 of 394 assertions exactly reproducible.** When a probabilistic interpreter
is added the number will fall, and the fall will be **visible and attributable** —
which is what the field was added for.

## Recommended next action

**A rule proposing both levels** — the concept, with its cases as constituent
assertions. It is the cheapest remaining improvement and it removes the one place
where `R3` is worse than `R1`.

**Then a rule that reads prose.** It is the only gap neither strategy reaches,
and it is where the honest case for probabilistic interpretation is strongest —
pattern-matching English deterministically is where a rule stops being a rule.

**Then, if still justified, the probabilistic interpreter**, measured against
`R3` over the same Mechanical Model.

**One caution worth carrying forward.** This session found that a ceiling claim
was premature. The Mechanical Model's vocabulary is now the next ceiling, and it
is equally easy to mistake for a limit of determinism: **a fact nobody extracted
is invisible to every interpreter, however good.**
