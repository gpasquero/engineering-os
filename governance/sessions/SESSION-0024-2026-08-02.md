## Decisions made

**`ADR-0066` — the metamodel defines `RelationshipType`, not `Relationship`.**

Do not model a relationship as an edge. Model it as a **relationship type**,
declaring domain, range, cardinality, constraints and semantics. Instances
already exist naturally in the graph and do not need an entity to give them
existence.

```text
RelationshipType → Compiler → ObjectProperty → Relationship instances
```

This preserves OWL compatibility while keeping Engineering OS
implementation-independent. It closes `FINDINGS.md` #1 and, as a consequence,
#5 — `typedBy` no longer exists, because under `RelationshipType` there is
nothing for an edge to be typed *by*.

**`ADR-0067` — the relationship is the design unit, not the entity.**

Entities are becoming lightweight; the semantics are moving into relationships,
constraints, cardinalities, inference and identity. Every entity specification
must now answer, before acceptance:

> **What new semantic relationship does this entity introduce that cannot
> already be expressed?**

If the answer is "none", the entity is probably redundant. This becomes part of
the Modeling Policy once Policies are implemented; until then the decision
enforces it.

**`ISSUE-0074` — metamodel simplification review, deferred by decision.** At
approximately 75% completion, identify entity pairs that can merge without losing
expressive power. `Dimension` / `DimensionSpecification` is the first candidate.
Explicitly *not* resolved now: **do not optimise prematurely, but do not preserve
accidental complexity either.**

## What was learned

### The `ADR-0067` question changed how the entities were written

Seven specifications were written under the new rule, and it did what it was
meant to do: each one opens by naming the single thing it adds.

- `Policy` — **normativity with a stated motivation.** `Invariant` constrains the
  modelled world; Policy constrains the engineering performed on it, and must
  name the Principle it derives from.
- `Workflow` — **ordering.** Every other relationship in the metamodel is
  timeless. Nothing else could say *first this, then that.*
- `AcceptanceRecord` — **authority.** The only entity that changes another
  entity's state.
- `ADR` — **supersession**, which is genuinely distinct from revision. A
  superseded ADR is not a stale revision; it is a correct record of what was
  decided then.
- `Issue` — **absent knowledge.** The only entity that represents something not
  known.

**`Issue` came closest to failing**, and its specification says so: it passes on
`blocks` and `defers-to` alone, because `resolved-by` is merely the inverse of
`ADR.resolves` and introduces nothing. Recording that honestly is more useful
than defending it.

### `ADR-0065` was tested and held

The operational family was written specifically as the test. All seven differ
structurally from every descriptive entity in the same three ways: owned by the
engineering process rather than a BoundedContext, carrying provenance about **who
acted**, and — the strongest signal — **two have lifecycles that are not
`ArtifactRevisionLifecycle`.**

`AcceptanceRecord` has **no lifecycle at all**: a record of an act cannot later
become a different act. `Issue` has its own — open, deferred, resolved — because
an issue is not accepted, it is answered.

A family boundary that predicts lifecycle divergence is describing something
structural, not imposed.

### Two new findings from the second checkpoint

**#7 — `RelationshipType` cannot express order.** `Workflow.sequences` is
ordered; its five fields have no notion of sequence. The relationship vocabulary,
introduced one session ago to be where relationship semantics live, cannot type
the first relationship that needed it. Found by writing the entity that needed
it, one session after the entity that should have provided it.

**#8 — two more relationships point at things that are not entities.**
`EngineeringGate.holds` points at *question*; `.produces` at *outcome*. This is
the third instance of the shape, after `Evidence.supports` and
`Relationship.typedBy`.

**The pattern is now worth naming: a relationship whose target is undefined is
the reliable signal of a missing entity — or of an entity that should not
exist.** Under `ADR-0067`, *question* probably introduces nothing a `Concept`
cannot express.

### An entity lived for exactly one session

`Relationship` was specified in `SESSION-0023` and withdrawn in `SESSION-0024`.
It is the shortest-lived artifact in the project, and **the first withdrawn
because building revealed the error rather than because analysis predicted it.**

The correction cost one file. Twenty sessions of prior analysis had never
surfaced the entity at all, let alone the problem with it.
