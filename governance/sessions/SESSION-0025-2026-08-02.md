## Decisions made

**`ADR-0068` — ordering is intrinsic or extrinsic, and needs no new construct.**

The instruction was to avoid solving the Workflow case and to determine whether
ordering is a property of a `RelationshipType`, a constraint attached to one, or
a separate semantic construct.

**It is none of the three, because "ordering" named two different things.**

The diagnostic test is mechanical:

> **Can the same target hold two different positions under two different
> sources?**

- **No → intrinsic.** The order is a property of the ordered entities.
  `EvidenceKind` is ranked by directness, identically wherever it is cited.
  Mechanism: a comparable property. `directnessRank` already was one.
- **Yes → extrinsic.** The same Skill is step 2 in one Workflow and step 5 in
  another, so the position is data on the association — and `ADR-0066` already
  requires an association carrying data to be an entity. Mechanism: reify.

`RelationshipType` is not extended. `Workflow --sequences--> Skill` becomes
`Workflow --has-step--> WorkflowStep --executes--> Skill`, and **both resulting
relationships are ordinary and unordered.** The ordering problem disappears
rather than being accommodated.

## What was learned

### The Specification/Instance pattern failed its second domain

This was the test, set up explicitly: if the pattern survived a second
independent domain, `Dimension` / `DimensionSpecification` should not yet be
merged; if it did not, the review had its first candidate.

**It did not survive.** `StateMachine` introduces no relationship its
specification does not already declare. Its specification says so in the
`ADR-0067` section, and the entity is retained only because `ISSUE-0074` exists
so removals happen as one pass.

The finding is larger than two merges. **The pattern is not
Specification/Instance — it is specification only, with the word "instance"
attached to the same thing twice.** Genuine instantiation exists in both domains
but not where the pattern put it:

| Domain | Specification | The real instance |
|---|---|---|
| Dimensions | `DimensionSpecification` | `DimensionAssignment` — already exists |
| State machines | `StateMachineSpecification` | an execution over time — deliberately outside the model |

If `RegistrySpecification` has the same shape, the conclusion is not that three
entities are redundant but that **`Specification` is a suffix applied where no
distinction exists.**

### The graphs found things no specification revealed

Generated three views and inspected only the metrics, as instructed. Five
findings came from the graphs alone:

**The metamodel has no reusable relationship vocabulary.** The relationship graph
reports **no relation used three or more times** across 28 properties and 21
classes. Every relationship is bespoke. For an architecture whose stated design
unit is the relationship (`ADR-0067`), this is the most consequential thing the
graphs showed — `RelationshipType` exists to hold a vocabulary that does not yet
exist.

**`governs` names three different relationships.** The entity graph reports it
three times, the relationship graph once. The discrepancy is the finding: the
ontology had already renamed one occurrence to `governsType` to avoid a clash,
and a rename forced by a clash is the signature of a term covering more than one
thing — *constrains normatively*, *controls the lifecycle of*, *may classify*.
**A candidate tenth terminology split, found by comparing two generated views.**

**`Dimension` is a pass-through, confirmed three ways.** Pendant and chain node in
the entity graph, chain node in the relationship graph. Drawn rather than
described, `DimensionAssignment --along--> Dimension --specified-by-->
DimensionSpecification` is visibly a two-hop path whose middle node adds nothing.

**`scoped-to` ×4 is `ContextualEntity`, rediscovered mechanically** — the same
repetition `FINDINGS.md` #3 found by hand, this time with nobody reading
anything.

**`Artifact` is where the two families touch.** Highest degree in both graphs,
reached constantly by operational entities. `ADR-0065` separated the families and
did not say where they meet; the graph answers.

### The class of discovery has changed

The first two checkpoints revealed **missing entities**. The third revealed a
**missing semantic construct**, and two more are already visible from the same
direction — transitions and conditions, both pointing at things that are not
entities and both extrinsic under `ADR-0068`'s test.

Missing entities indicate incomplete modelling. Missing constructs indicate the
metamodel is stabilising and the remaining work is **expressiveness rather than
coverage**.

### One entity was written to record that it should not exist

`state-machine.md` opens by stating it fails the `ADR-0067` test. Writing a
specification whose content is the argument against itself is a use of the format
nobody anticipated, and it is more useful than omission: the inventory link stays
valid and the finding is discoverable exactly where someone would look for it.
