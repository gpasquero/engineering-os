# SESSION-0006 — 2026-08-02

## Objective

Record the owner's answers to the four issues blocking M2 — all consequences of
making acceptance architectural — and create the repository's trust root.

## Decisions made

**`ADR-0020` — artifact taxonomy and revision lifecycle are independent.**
Supersedes `ADR-0018`. The lifecycle state `Authoritative` is renamed `Active`.
Taxonomy describes the *nature of an artifact*; lifecycle describes the
lifecycle of a *revision*. `Accepted` means the acceptance process completed;
`Active` means the accepted revision is the current governing one. Resolves
`ISSUE-0038`.

**`ADR-0021` — Acceptance Record specification.** First-class Authoritative
Artifacts under `governance/acceptance/`, with fourteen minimum fields.
Condition 3 is answered by **applicability**: where no deterministic validator
exists, none are applicable and the condition is satisfied. Resolves
`ISSUE-0041`.

**`ADR-0022` — bootstrap acceptance establishes the trust root.** Engineering OS
cannot retroactively invent history, so trust is bootstrapped explicitly by a
single record. The only retrospective acceptance ever permitted. Resolves
`ISSUE-0040`.

**`ADR-0023` — governance is self-hosting but never self-certifying.**
Governance policies are Authoritative Artifacts following the same lifecycle. A
policy cannot modify itself, and **the currently Active policy always governs
the acceptance of the next revision**. Resolves `ISSUE-0039`.

## Artifact created

**`ACCEPT-0001`** — the repository's trust root, covering the bootstrap corpus
at revision `2b6484f`. The first artifact in `governance/acceptance/`, and the
first artifact of any kind created outside the M1 document types.

## What was learned

**`Accepted` and `Active` are genuinely different, and the reason is sharp.** An
accepted revision may immediately become superseded by a newer accepted
revision. Acceptance is a completed event; being Active is a current status.
`ISSUE-0038` had flagged the ambiguity without seeing the reason for the
distinction.

**The condition-3 answer avoided inventing an exception.** The requirement reads
"all *applicable* deterministic validations". Where no validator exists, none
are applicable — so the condition is satisfied by the ordinary reading, not by a
waiver. This matters beyond the immediate case: a waiver would have needed
tracking, an expiry and later removal, and as tooling arrives checks now become
applicable automatically with no change to the acceptance model.

**A scope boundary was added to `ACCEPT-0001` that the instruction did not
state.** The instruction said the record covers the "complete M1–M2 corpus", but
M2 has not started. The record is therefore scoped to revision `2b6484f` and
explicitly cannot accept work created after it. Without that boundary a one-time
trust root would become a standing exemption from acceptance — the opposite of
its purpose. **This is an interpretation, flagged here and in the record itself
for review.**

**The repository now holds unaccepted work for the first time.** Everything
created after `2b6484f` — including `ACCEPT-0001` and the four ADRs above — is
`Under Review`, not `Active`. No acceptance record was created for it, because
the owner directed these decisions but has not reviewed their rendering into
ADR text. Creating a record asserting otherwise would have been the
self-certification `ADR-0023` prohibits.

This is the lifecycle working as designed, and it is the first time the
distinction has had teeth.

**`ADR-0023` needed no new concepts.** Governance policies reuse the artifact
taxonomy, the revision lifecycle and the Acceptance Record unchanged. That is
strong evidence those three abstractions were drawn at the right seams — a
self-referential case usually forces a special mechanism, and here it did not.

**Fifth supersession, and the acceptance chain is now three deep in two
sessions** (`ADR-0015` → `ADR-0018` → `ADR-0020`). Recorded in the ADR index as
honest evidence that this area is still settling.

## Issues opened

  new revision lifecycle. Nineteen ADRs are simultaneously marked
