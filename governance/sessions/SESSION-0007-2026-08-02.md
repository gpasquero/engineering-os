## Decisions made

**`ADR-0024` — the acceptance process terminates at the Acceptance Record.**
Acceptance Records are authoritative governance artifacts but are explicitly
excluded from recursive acceptance. A record derives its authority from the
decision it records. Not an exception — the base case. Resolves `ISSUE-0042`.

**`ADR-0025` — every state belongs to exactly one state machine.** There is no
global concept of "state". State names may coincide only when explicitly
namespaced, and **the same textual label never implies semantic equivalence
across machines**. `shared/vocabularies/` is grouped by state machine rather
than as one global list. Resolves `ISSUE-0043`.

## What was learned

**`ADR-0025` is the first decision that addresses a pattern rather than an
instance.** The project had caught the same class of defect three times —
"skill" (`ISSUE-0012`), "authoritative" (`ISSUE-0038`), and the status
vocabularies (`ISSUE-0043`) — and patched each individually. The answer
identifies the shared root cause: mixing independent state vocabularies as
though they described one thing. Future collisions become impossible by
construction rather than by vigilance.

**The nineteen-ADRs contradiction dissolved without renaming anything.**
`SESSION-0006` recorded it as a live inconsistency: nineteen ADRs marked
`status: accepted` while exactly one revision may be `Active`. Under `ADR-0025`
they were never the same state — `ADRLifecycle.Accepted` and
`ArtifactLifecycle.Active` belong to different machines. The three "overlapping"
vocabularies in `documentation-system.md` are retroactively legitimate; they
were always separate machines, merely never named as such.

That is worth noting as a pattern in itself: an apparent contradiction that
disappears once the right distinction is drawn, rather than being fixed.

**The rule reaches beyond this repository.** `ADR-0025` is a modeling rule for
the entire Engineering OS. The inherited prototypes require modeling lifecycles
and state machines in target domains, and this now governs how those are named.
The benefit to target systems is larger than the benefit to this repository's
own vocabulary.

**`ADR-0024` has a cost worth stating plainly.** Terminating the chain means an
Acceptance Record is the one artifact nothing else checks. A record naming a
reviewer who did not approve would be detectable only by asking that reviewer.
This is the irreducible cost of having a trust root, and it is why the
`reviewer` field must always name a real, askable party. Recorded as a negative
consequence rather than glossed.

**A naming tension was introduced.** `ADR-0025`'s example names the machine
`ArtifactLifecycle`, while `ADR-0020` was explicit that the lifecycle applies to
a **revision**, not an artifact — a distinction it made deliberately, since an
artifact with three revisions has one `Active` and two `Superseded`
simultaneously. The examples in `ADR-0025` were given as illustrations rather
than a definitive inventory, so this is recorded as `ISSUE-0044` rather than
resolved by preference.
